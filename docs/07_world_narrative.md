# 07. Narrative and world bible

Internal document. **It contains the answers.** Nothing below the heading
"What is true" may be stated in the game before the act it is assigned to.

Status: designed. No quest, dialogue or level exists.

## Premise

Eidolon runs on agreement. The oldest records say everything once agreed
with everything else, and call that the First Silence. Then something
disagreed, and the world broke into Severances: places where one rule of
existence holds differently. People who can impose a rule of their own are
called Sovereigns.

## What each layer of the world believes

| Layer | Who says it | The claim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Meridian Court | The Silence was paradise. Severances are wounds. Sovereigns are symptoms to be licensed. The Canon of Twelve is complete |
| 2 | The Pale Archive, privately | The Canon has been edited. Lines are struck in every copy, in the same hand |
| 3 | The Hollow Choir | The Silence never ended. It is underneath everything, and can be heard |
| 4 | The Crownless | Nobody remembers the Silence, so nobody is entitled to restore it |
| 5 | The Unbound | They do not speak in the future tense, and will not say why |

Each is partly right. The player meets them in this order and each later
layer makes an earlier scene mean something else.

## What is true

**The First Silence was not the beginning. It was an Edict.**

Before it, the world was plural: eighteen Roots, endlessly argued over by
people who could wield them, and a war that was ending everything. A group
of Sovereigns spoke the largest Edict ever spoken: *let all things agree.*
It worked. It was peace, and it was the end of anyone being anyone.

An Edict binds its author. The authors agreed along with everything else and
ceased to be able to will the Edict they were holding up. And an Edict's term
is counted in acts. In a world where nothing disagrees, nothing acts, so the
term never ran down. The Silence was a law with no living author and no way
to expire.

**The first thing that disagreed was a vow.** One of the authors, before the
Edict took hold, had made a promise to someone. A promise is a claim that the
future could go two ways. Inside the Silence that promise was the one thing
that could not agree, because keeping it required something to change. It
came due. That was the first act, and the Edict's count began to run.

The Silence is still running down. Each Severance is a place where it has
already lapsed and one of the six Roots it most needed to suppress has come
back. Axioms are not a new sickness. They are the old world's laws, returning
to people as the Edict fails. That is why some Axioms "remember" what came
before.

The Court did not strike six Roots from the Canon out of malice. The Court
is what remains of the authors' institution, and the six are the Roots that
ended the Silence once and could end what is left of it. Whether what is
left should end is the game's final question, and the game does not answer
it for the player.

**The title.** A vow falling is what ended the Silence, what Veyr's law
punishes, what the player's Vows risk, and what the protagonist witnesses in
the first scene.

## The Severances

Six, one for each struck Root (D-009). Each is a standing law with no living
author. In the engine a Severance is a regional law every entity inside is
subject to.

| Severance | Standing law | Root leaking | Look | Launch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Veyr, City of Crowns** (with the Hanging Courts above it) | A vow spoken before a witness binds | Ownership | Pale stone law-courts, verdigris bronze, hanging upper city chained to nothing | Yes. Slice |
| **The Mourning Wilds** | What is remembered here keeps happening | Memory | Birch forest where the habits of the dead still walk their paths | Yes |
| **Stillwater** | Distance follows attachment | Distance | A lake town where what you love is near and what you neglect drifts out of reach | Yes |
| **The Cathedral of Silence** | Nothing here may disagree | Absence | The largest intact piece of the First Silence. No sound. No conflict, until someone breaks it | Yes |
| **The Ashen Expanse** | What has begun here does not finish | Adaptation | A fire that ended three centuries ago and ash still falling; life that learned to live in burning | Expansion |
| **The Unwritten** | Nothing here has been decided | Possibility | Unfinished land: outlines, drafts, places that differ each visit | Endgame, repeatable expeditions |

The Hanging Kingdom of the original list is now Veyr's upper city: the
Crowns hang over the city they rule. One fewer region, and Veyr gains its
vertical landmark.

Every Severance gets: its law as a mechanic, one Sovereign, two enemy types
that exploit the law, one reason to return after a later Revelation, and no
collectible checklists.

## Factions

| Faction | Believes | Structure | Internal conflict | Wants from you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Meridian Court** | Agreement can be restored; Sovereigns must be licensed | Arbiters, Bailiffs, Notaries. Rank by seal | Restorers (bring the Silence back) vs Stewards (manage what is left) | Your licence, and your testimony sealed |
| **The Crownless** | No one owns a law, including their own | Cells, no ranks, debts of honour | Mutual aid vs insurrection | Your testimony made public |
| **The Pale Archive** | Everything must be recorded, especially what was struck | Copyists, Redactors, Keepers | Redactors (keep striking) vs those who want the lines restored | Your testimony, unedited, forever |
| **The Hollow Choir** | The Silence is still there and is merciful | Voices, graded by how long they can stay silent | Silence for all vs silence for oneself | To hear what you heard when the vow broke |
| **The Unbound** | A promise is the most dangerous thing a person can make | Travelling families; no contracts, no debts, no future tense | Whether to tell anyone what they know | Nothing. They want to know what you will promise |

The Unbound are no longer a second anti-authority faction (the original
overlapped with the Crownless). They are the people whose ancestors saw what
a vow did, and built a culture around never making one.

Membership changes dialogue, quests offered, who hunts you and how an area
is staffed. It locks no region and no Root.

## The slice story: "The First Severance"

1. **The oath.** You are hired to witness a contract in the Assize Quarter.
   One party breaks their vow in front of you. In Veyr that cannot happen.
   They die of it. The hall comes down. You get out one of three ways
   (`03_axiom_system.md`, awakening).
2. **Awakening.** The way you got out is the start of your Axiom. You choose
   between three candidates, each showing what you did to earn it.
3. **The Quarter.** You are the only witness. The Court wants you licensed
   before you testify. Maren Thole's Crownless want you to testify before
   you are licensed. Tove Hallam wants to know why the contract's text has a
   line struck through it that was not there when it was signed.
4. **What I Saw** (personal quest). You retrace the oath. Each step tests
   your Thesis against a situation it does not cover. Completing it is the
   Recognition Trial: Unveiling.
5. **The Hearing.** Aurel Voss summons you. The boss fight.
6. **Choice.** Take the licence or refuse it. The slice ends with the struck
   line restored on the page in front of you: it names a thirteenth Root.

## Tone and writing rules

* People in Eidolon talk like people with jobs. Law is their weather.
* No lore dumps. A record contradicts another record, and the player is left
  holding both.
* Every named character wants something from the protagonist and says so.
* Names: short, northern European and Levantine sounds mixed, no apostrophes.
